# Introduction

## Why a book, when there are so many videos?

There are thousands of videos about JavaScript. I've watched them too. The problem is that **videos are watched; books are done**. A video makes you feel productive in 40 minutes; a text forces you to wrestle with every idea, write the code yourself, and discover what you didn't actually understand.

This book complements videos; it doesn't replace them: for any difficult topic, the best recipe is *watch the video, then come here to truly understand it*.

## How was this book born?

I didn't write it in a weekend of inspiration. **It was born from my own study notes.** I really like JavaScript — I genuinely enjoy the language, I have fun wrestling with it — and as I studied it, my notes kept growing until I thought: "this isn't a note anymore, it's a chapter." This book is exactly that: my study notes, organized and explained, shared.

That has an honest consequence: this is not an academic book written from an ivory tower. It's material written by someone who codes, who made plenty of mistakes with this language, and who documented the journey from the most basic concepts to the most advanced ones.

## From using it to understanding it

Almost all material on the internet teaches you to *use* JavaScript: "write this and it works." This book goes somewhere else: **understanding how it works under the hood**.

- Not just *how* to declare a function, but what the lexical environment is and why closures work.
- Not just *async/await*, but what happens in the stack, the microtask queue, and the Event Loop.
- Not just *classes*, but the prototype chain underneath.

When you understand the mechanism, you stop memorizing syntax: the syntax becomes obvious.

## Who is it for?

For anyone who has already written some JavaScript — even a little — and wants to level up: from the language fundamentals to patterns, security, and the most modern APIs (ES2025–ES2026). I originally wrote it in Spanish because I know how valuable it is to rely less on mental translation; this English edition exists so the material can reach further.

## How to study it

1. **Read slowly**, with your editor open. Every example is meant to be run and broken by you.
2. **Do the exercises** at the end of each chapter.
3. **Come back**: the second read of a chapter you've already "mastered" is where real learning happens.
4. **Explain it**: if you can't explain a concept in your own words, it isn't yours yet.
